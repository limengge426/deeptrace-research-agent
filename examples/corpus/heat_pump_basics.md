# How heat pumps work

A heat pump does not burn fuel to make heat. It uses electricity to move heat from one place to another, the same way a refrigerator moves heat out of its food compartment.

An air-source heat pump pulls heat from outdoor air, even when that air feels cold, and releases it indoors. In summer the cycle runs in reverse and the same unit works as an air conditioner.

The core of the system is a refrigerant loop: the refrigerant evaporates at low temperature to absorb heat, a compressor raises its pressure and temperature, and a condenser releases that heat into the home.
