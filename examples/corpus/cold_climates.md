# Heat pumps in cold climates

Older air-source heat pumps struggled in freezing weather, which gave the technology a reputation as a mild-climate product.

Newer "cold-climate" models use variable-speed compressors and refrigerants designed to keep working at well below freezing, although their output and efficiency still decline as the temperature drops.

In very cold regions, installers often size the system for typical winter weather and keep a backup heat source, such as electric resistance strips or an existing furnace, for the coldest nights.
